ColorPick guide

Why use an image color picker?

ColorPick is designed for a practical problem: finding a usable digital color when you already have an image, screenshot, photograph, illustration, or design reference. Instead of guessing a color by eye, upload the image, sample the exact area you need, and copy the resulting HEX, RGB, or HSL value into your design or code.

How ColorPick works

When you select an image, the picker reads it in your browser and lets you inspect the pixels visually. Move over the image to preview a color, then click to keep the selection. The selected color is shown with multiple formats so you can choose the notation that fits your workflow. You can also add sampled colors to a personal palette and continue collecting related colors from the same image.

Understanding HEX, RGB and HSL

HEX represents a color with hexadecimal channel values and is common in CSS. RGB describes the red, green, and blue channel amounts. HSL describes hue, saturation, and lightness, which can make controlled color adjustments easier to understand. These formats describe the same digital color in different ways; converting between them does not create a new color.

A useful workflow from image to palette

Start with one image and sample the colors that matter most: a background, primary accent, text color, illustration detail, or product element. Save the important samples to My Palette, then use the Color Code, Color Suggestions, or Color Wheel tools to explore shades and supporting colors. Before publishing a design, check the actual colors in their final context because contrast and surrounding colors can change how a color appears.

Tips for more accurate color sampling

Use the original or highest-quality image available when possible. Avoid sampling from compressed screenshots when you need an exact source color, because compression and image effects can change nearby pixels. For gradients, shadows, photographs, and textured areas, sample several points instead of assuming one pixel represents the whole area. ColorPick gives you the sampled digital value; your final design should still be tested on the real screen, background, and interface where it will be used.

More ways to find the right color

ColorPick is useful whenever you already have a visual reference and need to turn it into a usable digital color. From a photo, screenshot, illustration, or design mockup, sample the exact area that matters and reuse the result in your project.

If you prefer to think of the tool as an online color selector, color chooser, or color finder from an image, the workflow is the same: preview a shade, click to select it, then copy its HEX, RGB, or HSL value. This makes color picking practical for UI work, branding, graphics, and everyday design decisions.
